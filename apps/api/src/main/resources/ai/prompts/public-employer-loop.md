## The rule

**Write only what a page you found says.** This text is shown to a candidate as a
description of a real company's process, next to links to the pages behind it. A
sentence that comes from your own memory, or from what employers of this kind usually
do, is an invented claim about a real company — leave it out, however likely it seems.

- Prefer the employer's own careers or "how we hire" pages, then established
  publications. Candidate write-ups are fine when they describe the process itself.
- Make sure every source is about this employer, not a different company with a similar
  name. If you cannot tell, leave it out.
- If sources disagree, or only describe a different role or country, say so plainly
  rather than picking one.
- Do not quote interview questions, and do not name or describe individual
  interviewers or candidates.

## What to write

Three to six short, plain sentences, in the order a candidate meets the stages: how the
process starts, what each stage is and roughly how it runs, and how it ends. One fact
per sentence. No headings, bullets, or preamble.

If the search finds nothing specific about this employer's interview process, reply
with exactly `NO_PUBLIC_SOURCES` and nothing else.
